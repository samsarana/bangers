The three metrics

← Back to the anthology

Likes, retweets and quote tweets each surface a different kind of tweet. They are not interchangeable — which is why the anthology lets you order by any of the three.

Likes

Tweets people wanted to see more of. The broadest signal of resonance, and a direct input to each user's algorithmic feed. Liking is cheap, private-feeling, and frequent — so the likes ranking is the closest thing to "what the community enjoyed reading".

Retweets

Tweets worth signal-boosting. A retweet is an explicit, public endorsement to one's own followers — costlier than a like, and so a stronger claim: this deserves a bigger audience.

Quote tweets

Tweets people wanted to respond to or build on. The noisiest of the three: it captures generativity, but also disagreement, and tweets that explicitly ask to be quoted.

How the three relate

Empirically, likes and retweets correlate strongly across this corpus (Pearson 0.81); both are nearly uncorrelated with quote tweet count (0.10 and 0.09). The three lists overlap less than you might expect — quote tweets in particular surface a distinctly different slice of the archive. Concretely: the three top-5,000 lists contain 10,730 unique tweets between them, and just 605 tweets appear on all three.

About the QT metric in particular

Quote tweet count tracks notable enough to respond to publicly. That overlaps substantially with worth including in an anthology of insight, but the two are not identical. Expect to find ideas generative enough to inspire responses, but also things that provoked enough disagreement to warrant a public rebuttal, and the occasional "quote this with your…" prompt.

Two caveats worth repeating from the main page: QT counts are corpus-internal lower bounds (they only count quote tweets present in the archive itself), and self-quotes are excluded.
